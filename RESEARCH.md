# Research proposal — discussion draft

## Intended contribution

Investigate whether explicit, student-correctable adaptation adds value beyond ordinary AI tutoring. The proposed contribution is an inspectable adaptation process and a comparison separating knowledge-based adaptation from barrier-informed adaptation. Novelty must be established by literature review; it is not assumed.

## Questions and hypotheses

| ID | Question | Proposed hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RQ1 | Does adaptation improve understanding? | Adaptive conditions improve post-lesson performance when baseline knowledge is accounted for. |
| RQ2 | Does optional barrier information add value? | Knowledge-plus-barrier adaptation improves outcomes over knowledge-only adaptation. |
| RQ3 | How do students experience adaptation? | Student control improves perceived helpfulness without increasing pressure. |
| RQ4 | Where does adaptation fail? | Failures can be categorized as assessment, planning, content, or interaction errors. |

These are testable hypotheses, not findings. A small pilot may support feasibility conclusions rather than efficacy claims.

## Initial scope

One curriculum unit; equivalent fractions is the replaceable development example. Choose target age and setting with the professor before recruitment. Start with fictional sessions. Exclude diagnosis, therapy, camera-based emotion recognition, and hidden mental health profiling.

## Proposed comparison

Use the same approved content, model version, tools, objectives, and time budget across:
1. Fixed tutor: predetermined lesson sequence.
2. Knowledge-adaptive tutor: sequence changes based on quiz evidence.
3. Knowledge-and-barrier-adaptive tutor: adds optional self-report and student choice.

A randomized between-participant design is a candidate. If resources cannot support three conditions, agree on a two-condition feasibility pilot and defer the ablation. Do not interpret practice gains from repeating the same lesson across conditions as treatment effects.

## Literature review work package

Search primary research on intelligent tutoring systems, adaptive learning, formative assessment, agency, motivation, and LLM tutor evaluation. Record databases, queries, dates, inclusion criteria, and exclusions. The evidence matrix should include citation, participants, intervention, comparator, outcomes, limitations, and relevance. Distinguish peer-reviewed research, preprints, and product claims. No completed evidence review or invented references are included here.

## Study readiness gate

Before recruitment, agree on eligibility, consent/assent as applicable, institutional review requirements, withdrawal, data handling, support procedures, instruments, analysis, and sample-size justification. Obtain required approvals; this document is not approval. Participation must not determine access to educational support.

## Deliverables

Research protocol, literature matrix, versioned prototype, fictional scenario suite, evaluation scripts, approved study materials if applicable, reproducible analysis, and a final report/poster with limitations. Authorship and institutional ownership require team agreement.
