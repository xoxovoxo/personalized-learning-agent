# Data and human support design

A project design policy, not a legal or clinical protocol. Review procedures with the professor and relevant institutional staff for the intended learners and setting.

## Development

Use fictional profiles and synthetic answers. Public artifacts may include prompts, appropriately licensed curriculum, test cases, schemas, and non-sensitive development results. Never commit identities, contact details, credentials, actual student responses, or sensitive disclosures.

## Proposed minimal events

Session pseudonym, condition, item ID, correctness, strategy ID, override, prompt/curriculum version, and latency. Collect optional barrier selections only when justified and consented. Exclude free-text transcripts by default. Pseudonymous data is not necessarily anonymous.

## Before real data collection

Specify purpose, access roles, approved storage, retention, deletion, consent/assent as applicable, withdrawal, and incident handling. Review model-provider data practices. Store identity linkage separately. Obtain required institutional approvals; do not claim regulatory compliance or invent a universal retention period.

## Student controls

Allow personal questions to be skipped. Explain storage and access. Provide session reset and a deletion-request process consistent with the approved protocol. Do not secretly infer diagnoses, disability, emotion, or family circumstances. Do not use sensitive disclosures to determine grades or educational eligibility.

## Human support

The tutor may acknowledge difficulty, offer educational adjustments, and present reviewed human-support options. It must not diagnose, treat, promise to solve mental health conditions, or encourage dependence. Do not promise absolute confidentiality or automatic monitoring.

Before real use, define a setting-appropriate urgent-disclosure procedure, reviewed resources, responsible humans, availability, and limitations. Do not invent contact numbers or claim outreach occurred without an authorized, confirmed workflow. Ordinary disclosures should not automatically be shared outside the agreed process.

Assign human owners for curriculum errors, privacy incidents, and support review. Pause a pilot after a critical issue and resolve it before resuming. Public bug reports must use synthetic examples.
