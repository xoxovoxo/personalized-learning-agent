# Proposed 12-week roadmap

Timing is relative to team kickoff, not a committed deadline. Revise after professor review.

| Phase | Window | Deliverable | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | Weeks 1–2 | Questions, literature matrix, charter | Learners/topic agreed; comparator recorded |
| Content and design | Weeks 3–4 | Lesson, item bank, contracts, study draft | Content reviewed; items mapped to objectives; privacy decisions recorded |
| First prototype | Weeks 5–6 | Complete local session | Three fictional scenarios; skipping and override work |
| AI integration | Weeks 7–8 | Adapter, grounded tutor, comparison modes | Shared content/model; controlled fallbacks; versions logged |
| Evaluation readiness | Weeks 9–10 | Scenario results, materials, analysis plan | Critical errors fixed; human-study approvals if applicable |
| Study or feasibility report | Weeks 11–12 | Approved pilot or fictional-session report | Reproducible findings, limitations, report and demo |

## Milestones

- [ ] M0: Team agrees on scope and roles.
- [ ] M1: Curriculum and assessment review complete.
- [ ] M2: End-to-end fictional demonstrator works.
- [ ] M3: Comparison conditions reproducibly selectable.
- [ ] M4: Educational correctness, privacy, and support review complete.
- [ ] M5: Approved pilot or feasibility-only report delivered.

## Stretch scope

Additional topics, retention study, teacher review dashboard, accessibility options, and longitudinal adaptation. Persistent storage and larger studies need separate approval and resource plans.

## Dependencies and scope control

Do not recruit while review is pending. If approvals or recruitment are unavailable, deliver an engineering feasibility report with fictional data and no efficacy claims. If correctness is unreliable, restrict output to approved explanations. Defer dashboards and additional subjects before reducing evaluation quality.
